# Trade Life Cycle

## Introduction

The trade life cycle refers to the sequence of activities involved in executing and completing a financial market transaction, from placing an order to final settlement.

## Key Stages

### 1. Order Initiation
A client or trader initiates an order to buy or sell a financial instrument.

### 2. Trade Execution
The order is executed on an exchange or through an appropriate trading venue.

### 3. Trade Capture
The transaction details are recorded in the relevant trading systems.

### 4. Trade Confirmation
The counterparties verify key trade details, such as the instrument, quantity, price, and settlement date.

### 5. Clearing
The obligations of the buyer and seller are calculated and prepared for settlement. The process depends on the market and clearing arrangements.

### 6. Settlement
The securities and funds are exchanged according to the agreed settlement terms.

### 7. Reconciliation
Records are compared across relevant systems to identify and resolve discrepancies.

## Importance of Trade Life Cycle Management

- Supports accurate transaction processing.
- Helps identify discrepancies and operational risks.
- Promotes timely settlement.
- Improves record keeping and operational controls.

## Learning Summary

Understanding the trade life cycle helps explain how financial transactions move through front-office execution and back-office processing activities.
